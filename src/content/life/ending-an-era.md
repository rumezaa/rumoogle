> Put a track athlete on a track, and they'll run relative to their peers. When you're running with fast people, you run faster.

From the ages of 16-18, with confidence, I can say I was running fast. All I had around me were people who were running faster. That pushed me in many ways to see what I could become if I moved farther than I thought I could.

That hasn't changed since I started university, but my track moved a bit. I've been so privileged to have surrounded myself with the most creative individuals I've ever met these past few months. The most human ones. Naturally, as with the growing company of creatives, some of my interests began to shift too.

This came in the form of taking time off of my technical pursuits to focus on building a philanthropic grant, in which I gave five grand of my own money to send people on adventures all over the world. I also started hosting events, and most recently, I hosted a 600 person builder film festival (The Canon Event).

All of these experiences, coupled, showed me a different direction I could take my life, my passions and my interests. I know I have always wanted to build things for people, but for a time I was curating for people instead. In my head, this means bringing people together, which is what I hoped my technical pursuits would be able to create, but through these initiatives, I brought people together.

After we all took that bow November 15 on the stage for the Canon Event, I knew that I would be saying goodbye to this phase of my life. The weeks that led up to the event showed me the sheer amount of emailing, delusion and importance of a good freaking team it takes to put something up at scale.

All throughout the time I was putting this and The Archive cohort together, I realized I wanted to be on the other end. I don't want to be the one curating anymore. I want to go back to being the person with the thing worth curating.

I remember particularly talking to Mom about quitting The Archive this summer because I felt so drained from having to juggle putting it on (essentially by myself) while also learning how to live alone 4,000 km away and still wanting to continue building side projects at the same time. I had the thought that I was wasting my time investing my energy into something I just wasn't attached to anymore, but in those moments I realized the importance of having the opportunity to figure out it wasn't for me sooner rather than later. If anything, taking that risk to start this community humanized my goals in a way I don't think any amount of project building could. Like, how often is it that you get the opportunity to help reunite someone with their birth family, or fund a risk to learn a language in a completely new country all by yourself? It's still crazy to me that I have friends I can count on who would show up for 13 hours to help me put on events, and people that believe in these whimsical visions.

For a while I was running track with people on a different track. A wonderful, important track, but I realize I want to belong elsewhere. I just have to create that belonging and start running the race I wanted to be a part of at the start.

soooo that's my next adventure. i know it won't lose the whimsy or people aspect of it, but it will be different. i now want to focus on increasing my technical domain as I've found my "vertical" (finally, after years of interning). but i'm approaching these ambitions and goals not as a performance but like an adventure, in great part due to the company I've had of my beautiful, talented, creative friends.

I love what I am capable of doing, but I'm excited for what I want to be doing :)
